Structural inspections

Cracks · Movement · Defects

What is a structural inspection?

A structural inspection is a visual assessment carried out by a structural engineer to investigate concerns relating to the stability or structural condition of a building.

Unlike a general Building Survey, our inspection focuses specifically on the structure — how the building is behaving, the likely cause of any defects and whether further investigation, monitoring or remedial work is required.

We offer three types of structural inspection depending on the reason for the assessment:

  • Specific Structural Inspection (SSI) — a focused inspection of a particular structural concern, such as cracking, movement, a bowing wall or sagging floor. This is often requested following a Building Survey where a specific issue has been identified.
  • General Structural Inspection (GSI) — a broader assessment where there are several structural concerns or where the overall structural condition of the property needs to be considered.
  • Mortgage Structural Inspection (MSI) — an inspection carried out where a mortgage lender or valuer has identified a potential structural concern and requires a structural engineer's report before proceeding.

If you are unsure which inspection you require, send us a copy of your Building Survey, mortgage valuation or details of your concerns and we can advise on the most appropriate inspection.

Specific Structural Inspection (SSI)

A Specific Structural Inspection (SSI) is suitable where there is one clearly identified structural concern or localised area requiring investigation. It is commonly requested following a Building Survey where the surveyor has identified a particular structural issue and recommended further assessment by a structural engineer.

This might include a particular crack, bowing wall, movement around an opening, sagging floor or another isolated structural concern.

The inspection is limited to the area or issue agreed beforehand and provides a proportionate way of obtaining professional structural advice without commissioning an inspection of the entire property.

Specific Structural Inspection (SSI)£495–£795 + VAT

Fees are based on a single identified structural concern and include a site inspection and written report. The final fee will depend on the location of the property and the nature and complexity of the issue. A fixed quotation will be provided before instruction.

General Structural Inspection (GSI)

A General Structural Inspection (GSI) provides a broader visual assessment where there are several structural concerns or where the overall structural condition of a property needs to be considered.

A GSI may be instructed by a homeowner, purchaser, building surveyor or other property professional. It is often requested following a Building Survey where several structural concerns have been identified, or where a broader assessment by a structural engineer has been recommended rather than investigation of one specific defect.

Depending on the property and the concerns raised, we may assess cracking and movement internally and externally, walls and openings, floors, visible structural elements, previous alterations and other signs of structural distress.

We will consider the pattern and likely cause of any movement and advise whether the defects appear historic, ongoing or require further investigation.

General Structural Inspection (GSI)£795–£1,250 + VAT

Fees include a site inspection and written report and will vary depending on the size, location and complexity of the property. Larger properties or buildings with more extensive structural concerns may require an individual quotation. A fixed quotation will always be provided before instruction.

Mortgage structural inspection

Mortgage Valuations · Lender Reports

A Mortgage Structural Inspection (MSI) is typically required where a mortgage lender or their valuer has identified a potential structural concern and requests further assessment before proceeding with a mortgage offer or releasing funds.

This may relate to cracking, historic or suspected movement, structural alterations, distortion, previous repairs or another issue identified during the mortgage valuation.

The inspection focuses on the concerns raised by the lender or valuer. We will assess the relevant structural elements, consider the likely cause and significance of any defects and provide a written report setting out our findings and recommendations.

Where possible, clients should provide a copy of the lender or valuer's requirements before the inspection so that the report can be prepared specifically in response to the matters they have raised.

Mortgage Structural Inspection (MSI)£595–£895 + VAT

Fees include a site inspection and written report and will vary depending on the location of the property, the scope of the lender's requirements and the complexity of the structural concerns. A fixed quotation will be provided before instruction.

Where the lender specifically requires the inspection or report to be undertaken or signed by a Chartered Engineer, please provide their requirements when requesting a quotation.
