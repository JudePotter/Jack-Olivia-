Structural Inspections · Mortgage Reports

A structural inspection is a visual assessment carried out by a structural engineer to investigate concerns relating to the stability or structural condition of a building.

Unlike a general building survey, our inspection focuses specifically on the structure, how the building is behaving, the likely cause of any defects and whether further investigation or remedial work is required.

You may benefit from a structural inspection if you have noticed cracking, movement, bowing walls, uneven or sagging floors, sticking doors or windows, distortion or other changes to your property.

You may also have been referred to a structural engineer following an RICS building survey, mortgage valuation or inspection by another property professional.

An RICS building survey considers the overall condition of a property and may cover roofing, damp, drainage, services, finishes and other aspects of the building.

A structural inspection by a structural engineer is more specialist and concentrates on the load bearing structure and structural behaviour of the building.

It is common for a building surveyor to identify something potentially structural and recommend that a structural engineer investigates it in greater detail.

Our standard structural inspections are visual and non-intrusive.

If our inspection identifies significant structural movement, we will explain the nature and extent of the issue within our report. This will include a clear explanation of the structural defects identified and their likely cause, an assessment of whether the movement appears historic, ongoing or requires further investigation, recommendations for any further investigations, monitoring or remedial works considered necessary, guidance on the appropriate next steps, and a written structural report setting out our observations, conclusions and recommendations.

The effect on your mortgage application will depend on the nature and severity of the movement, the recommended remedial works and your individual lender's requirements — including the severity of the movement, any proposed repairs or investigations, the availability of buildings insurance and each lender's own lending criteria.

We cannot guarantee whether a lender will approve a mortgage following our inspection, as the final lending decision remains with the mortgage provider.
